Non-invasive blood-loss monitoring during endoscopic urology

Team project with clinicians · Hôpital Tenon, Sorbonne Université · 2025–2026

Problem. Blood loss is hard to quantify in real time during endoscopic procedures, because it is mixed with irrigation fluid.

What I did. This was teamwork with no single lead. Together we agreed on a rough block diagram of what the system needed; I then designed the electronic circuit itself, sizing it and drawing the schematic in EasyEDA around an STM32 microcontroller, an AS7265x spectral sensor, an SLF3S flow sensor and a LED driver. Teammates wrote the embedded code and the bill of materials; because I was drawing the final, more complex circuit, I guided the component choices.

What I learned. Turning a clinical need into measurable physical quantities, and translating a team’s block diagram into a concrete, buildable circuit.

AdaptStep — a self-calibrating threshold for Digi’Feet, a connected insole for the diabetic foot

Team leader of AdaptStep (5 students) · Sorbonne Université, Biomechanics module · 2025–2026 · Highest grade awarded

Problem. Before meeting the startups, I had studied the diabetic foot on my own and written the technical dossier below, around a problem I chose. Startups then gave us a brief related to that work, including Advanced Care Technology, whose product is the Digi’Feet insole; I formed the team (AdaptStep) and we worked on it. The core issue: a single pressure threshold is applied to every diabetic patient, yet the same pressure can be tolerated by one person and dangerous for another.

What I did. As team leader I wrote the specification and had it signed, assigning a role to each member. We then delivered code, results, a complete report and a full final presentation, covering a comparative study of sensor technologies for an instrumented insole, and the concept of an adaptive model that builds a personal baseline per foot zone, adjusts it with clinical covariates such as BMI and foot type, and updates over time only when data are reliable (sensor-confidence and posture-quality checks).

Result. On simulated clinical profiles, the personalised threshold clearly outperformed a fixed one on specificity and discrimination, with statistically significant differences.

Limits. Validation is on synthetic data only; real clinical validation is the necessary next step.

Connected insoles for the diabetic foot — individual technical dossier

Biomechanics module · Sorbonne Université, M1 · 2026

Question. To what extent do the neurovascular damage of diabetes compromise foot balance in static stance, and which technological and therapeutic approaches can compensate?

What I did. I worked through foot anatomy and static load distribution; how neuropathy, stiffened tissue and deformities converge into pressure hotspots; offloading and proprioceptive rehabilitation; the architecture of a connected insole (pressure sensors, microcontroller, BLE, power, sensor placement, processing pipeline, biofeedback); clinical evidence; and the regulatory path (EU MDR classification, ISO 13485, IEC 62304, IEC 60601-1) and reimbursement barriers. I also listed current limits: sensor drift, battery life, adherence and patient heterogeneity. This dossier came first; startups then gave us a brief related to it, which became the AdaptStep project.

3D segmentation of neuronal circuits — AxonEM challenge

Image-processing project · Sorbonne Université · 2025–2026

What I did. I analysed the challenge and the state of the art, and studied the flood-filling-network approach and its multi-run consensus and agglomeration steps. I prototyped the pre-processing and segmentation pipeline in Python on a subset of the data.

Limits, honestly. Storage limits prevented a full run, so I did not reach the final plausibility step. The main lesson is how heavy iterative neural reconstruction is on memory and compute, and why lighter successor methods matter.

Radio-frequency absorption (SAR) in head tissues

Bioelectromagnetics project · Sorbonne Université · 2026

I modelled head exposure to mobile-network antennas: from a homogeneous sphere to a multilayer skin/bone/brain model with attenuation with depth, several frequencies and antennas. I fitted a Debye dielectric model to measured tissue data, derived an equivalent R/C circuit, and compared results with ICNIRP reference levels and a real base-station case.

Aspirations and well-being — a sociology study

CMI sociology module · CY Tech, L2 · individual project

Question. How do a country’s socio-economic and cultural context shape individuals’ aspirations and well-being?

What I did. I reviewed the literature (gender, social origin, economic context), chose the question and five hypotheses myself, then ran a semi-structured online questionnaire (35 responses, mostly students aged 18–25) and four focus groups.

Result. Four hypotheses were supported, for example that well-being and ambition are tied to cultural and economic environment, and that women more often seek financial stability. One was rejected: aspirations did not shift generation after generation from financial stability toward “following a dream”.

Limits. Small, student-heavy sample, so the findings are indicative, not general.
